Personal Development

My reflection on lessons from The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason.

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

In The Richest Man in Babylon, George S. Clason uses Arkad to explain seven principles for improving a person's financial situation. What stood out to me is that most of the principles are simple, but they require discipline and consistency. The chapter made me realize that earning money is only part of becoming financially stable. How I save, spend, invest, and improve myself is just as important.

1. Start Thy Purse to Fattening

The first cure teaches that a person should save at least one-tenth of everything they earn instead of spending their entire income. The idea is to pay yourself first before using the rest of your money for expenses.

I can apply this by automatically putting aside a portion of any money I receive, whether it is from a salary, freelance work, or another source of income. Instead of waiting to see what is left at the end of the month, I should treat saving as something that has to be done first. I believe this would help me develop better financial discipline and give me money that I can eventually invest.

2. Control Thy Expenditures

This lesson is about separating actual needs from wants. A person can always find something else to spend money on, so without a budget, expenses can continue increasing no matter how much money they earn.

For me, this means paying more attention to where my money is going. There are things I enjoy spending money on, but I also have to recognize that not everything I want is necessary. I can still enjoy myself while setting limits and making sure my spending does not interfere with my savings or future goals.

3. Make Thy Gold Multiply

The third cure teaches that saved money should eventually be put to work instead of simply sitting there. The goal is for money to generate additional money through sensible investments.

This lesson connects directly to something I want to learn more about, which is investing. Instead of only saving money in an account, I would like to understand investments such as stocks and other long-term options so that my savings have the potential to grow. I also understand from the book that I should learn before investing instead of putting money into something just because other people say it will make money.

4. Guard Thy Treasures from Loss

This cure focuses on protecting the money that has already been earned. It emphasizes being careful with investments and avoiding situations where there is a high possibility of losing the original amount.

I can apply this by researching an investment before committing money to it. I should not invest because of hype, pressure from other people, or promises of quick returns. I would rather take the time to understand what I am investing in and get information from people who actually have experience in that area.

5. Make of Thy Dwelling a Profitable Investment

This lesson encourages people to work toward making their home and living situation financially beneficial instead of allowing housing costs to become an unnecessary burden.

At my stage in life, owning a home may not be an immediate goal, but the lesson is still useful. It makes me think about long-term planning and the importance of eventually owning assets instead of constantly paying for things without building anything for myself. It also reminds me that major purchases should be planned carefully instead of being made impulsively.

6. Insure a Future Income

The sixth cure is about preparing for the future, including times when a person may no longer be able to work or when their family may need financial support.

This made me think beyond earning money right now. It is easy to focus only on immediate goals, but financial planning should also include retirement, emergencies, and family responsibilities. I can start by building savings and later investing consistently so that I am not completely dependent on my next salary.

7. Increase Thy Ability to Earn

The final cure focuses on improving yourself so that your earning potential increases. Arkad encourages people to gain knowledge, improve their skills, and become better at what they do.

This is probably one of the lessons I relate to the most because I am studying Computer Science and already working in software development. Technology changes quickly, so I cannot depend only on what I currently know. Learning new technologies, improving my programming skills, gaining experience working with development teams, and becoming stronger at problem solving can all increase the opportunities available to me in the future.

The Five Laws of Gold

The Five Laws of Gold take the ideas about saving and investing even further. The chapter shows that simply having money does not guarantee that a person will keep it. The story of Nomasir demonstrates that knowledge and good financial judgement are more valuable than being given money without knowing how to manage it.

First Law of Gold

The first law teaches that money comes more easily to someone who consistently saves at least one-tenth of their earnings for their future.

This reinforces the first cure from the previous chapter. What I took from it is that building wealth does not necessarily require starting with a large amount of money. The habit of consistently saving matters more. Even if I start small, I can continue increasing the amount as my income grows.

Second Law of Gold

The second law says that money works for the person who puts it into profitable use. In other words, money that is invested wisely can create more money over time.

I can apply this by learning how different investments work rather than leaving all of my savings unused. I want my money to eventually generate returns of its own. However, I also understand that investing should be done with patience instead of expecting immediate results.

Third Law of Gold

The third law teaches that money is safer when the owner seeks advice from people who are knowledgeable and experienced in handling investments.

This is important to me because there is a lot of financial information online, and not all of it is trustworthy. Before making larger investment decisions, I would want to learn from credible sources and people who understand the type of investment I am considering.

Fourth Law of Gold

The fourth law warns against putting money into businesses or investments that a person does not understand.

This lesson is especially important today because people regularly hear about new investment opportunities, cryptocurrencies, businesses, and different ways of making money online. I should not invest simply because something looks profitable. If I cannot explain how the investment works, where the return comes from, and what the risks are, then I should probably learn more before putting my money into it.

Fifth Law of Gold

The fifth law warns against investments that promise unrealistic returns and against trusting schemes or people who claim that wealth can be made quickly and easily.

This lesson reminds me not to become impatient with the process of building wealth. A promise of very high returns can be tempting, especially when someone wants to make money quickly, but that can also lead to poor decisions. I would rather build wealth gradually through saving, investing, and increasing my income than risk losing what I have trying to get rich overnight.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from The Richest Man in Babylon is that financial success has more to do with habits than with suddenly earning a large amount of money. Before reading these chapters, it would be easy to think that becoming financially successful mainly depends on getting a high-paying job. The book made me look at it differently. A person can earn a lot and still have nothing if they spend everything they receive, while someone who manages their money carefully can gradually build financial security.

The principles that stand out to me the most are saving a portion of everything I earn, increasing my ability to earn, and being careful about where I invest. These are things I can realistically start applying while I am still young. As I progress in my career in technology, I want to avoid increasing my spending every time my income increases. Instead, I want part of that additional income to go toward savings and investments.

I also learned that knowledge is extremely important when dealing with money. The Five Laws of Gold show that having money without understanding how to manage it can cause someone to lose it quickly. The story of Nomasir especially demonstrates how financial wisdom can eventually become more valuable than the money a person initially receives.

Overall, the book changed the way I think about building wealth. My goal should not only be to make more money, but to keep part of what I earn, control how I spend it, make it grow, protect it from unnecessary risks, and continuously improve myself so that my earning potential increases. Those are habits I can carry throughout my life rather than something that only applies when I start earning a large salary.

Source

Clason, G. S. (1926). The Richest Man in Babylon.
